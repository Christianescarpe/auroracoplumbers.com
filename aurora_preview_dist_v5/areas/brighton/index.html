City of Brighton

Plumber Brighton CO

Brighton lets an emergency water-heater or service-line repair start before the paperwork, if you apply the next business day and you do not cover the work.

Which repair → Quote checklist →

Emergency, then the permit

The permitting page says emergency repairs and replacements can be done for water heaters, furnaces, boilers, heat pumps, and a water supply or sewer service. You must apply by the next business day, and the work cannot be concealed. Email 1stop@brightonco.gov about the emergency and apply in the portal. After a normal permit is paid, the stamped plans and the permit card have to be printed and available to the inspector. The building counter is 303-655-2017. The homeowner inspection line is 303-655-2151. Those are city addresses and numbers, not a referral line.

Do not hide the repair

Covering a sewer repair before the inspector sees it breaks the emergency exception. That is the opposite of Aurora's instruction to keep permitted work open, and it is Brighton's own sentence. The page does not say who owns the lateral. Ask the city's utility before you accept a street patch. A planned, non-emergency water heater still needs the permit. It is not an excuse to skip it.

A slow drain is drain cleaning until a camera shows a broken sewer line. A tank swap is water heater replacement. Confirm the permit office and the utility for this address before the work is closed up.

Key Clarifications

Questions to settle first

Can I replace a water heater in Brighton before the permit is issued?

Only as an emergency, and only if you apply the next business day and leave the work open to inspect. The page lists water heaters and water or sewer service repairs in that exception. A planned replacement is a normal permit. Do not bury the pipe and call it an emergency later.

Where does the inspector look?

At the open repair, with the stamped plans and permit card printed from the portal once the permit is paid. The homeowner inspection line is 303-655-2151. Covering the work removes the exception the city described. Ask them to write that limit into the quote before you compare it with another company.

Is the sewer the city's to the house?

The permitting page does not say that. It tells you how to permit the repair. Ownership is a utility question for the address. Do not use Aurora's tap rule or a contractor's sidewalk rule until Brighton confirms it. Ask them to write that limit into the quote before you compare it with another company.
