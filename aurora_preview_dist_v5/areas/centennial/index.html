City of Centennial · not Aurora Water

Plumber Centennial CO

Centennial does not run your water or your sewer. The City's own utilities page says so. The building department does review permits, and a residential water heater is not reviewed like a new store. Those two facts change the call you make before a plumber quotes the job.

Find the district before you argue about the pipe

The City tells residents to use Centennial Property Search to identify the electric and gas provider and the water and sanitation district for the address. Several districts serve the city. A rule you heard about an Aurora tap, or about a Denver main, is not automatically the district on your bill. Trash and utilities also notes that much of Centennial's water comes through Denver Water distributors, and it points to Denver Water's lead program. That program is about eligible lines. It is not a reason to buy a private replacement before you check the address.

Because the district, not the City, owns the utility question, this page will not tell you that you maintain the pipe to a tap. That sentence is Aurora's. Ask the district named on your property record.

What the building counter treats as one-stop

Centennial's review schedule lists water heaters, gas lines, and residential sewer line replacements among one-stop permits that need no plan review. It states one business day for that group. New commercial work and new houses are listed at 15 business days after the plan-review fee is paid. Commercial alterations are listed at 10. The page says timelines for first comments start when a complete submittal is in, and it encourages a pre-submittal meeting. The Building Division number printed there is 303-754-3321. Those are the City's targets, not a promise from this website that your heater will be approved tomorrow.

A one-stop water heater is still not a tankless conversion in another town's worksheet, and it is not a multifamily project. If the job is a new building or a commercial tenant finish, you are on the longer list. For an Aurora address, ignore this schedule and use Aurora water heater replacement. For a house that might be Castle Rock, the tankless review is different again: Castle Rock.

How that should show up in the quote

Ask which district serves the address, and for the sewer or water repair, ask that district who maintains the line before you fund a street cut. Ask whether the building permit is the one-stop residential item or a reviewed commercial set. Ask who pulls it. A sewer repair quoted as if Aurora Water owned the main in the street is the wrong city. Camera first, then the district's rule.

Questions to settle first

Does the City of Centennial fix the water or sewer line?

The City says it does not provide water or sewer. Districts do. Use the property search on the City's site to see which district covers the address before you accept a story about who owns the pipe. Aurora's tap rule is for Aurora Water customers. It is not a Centennial ordinance.

How long does a water heater permit take?

The published schedule puts a residential water heater in the one-stop group and states one business day. That clock is the City's target for a complete submittal, not a guarantee, and it is not the 15-business-day line used for a new house or a new commercial building. Ask the contractor which list this permit is on.

Is a Centennial mailing address always this city?

No. Confirm the parcel is inside Centennial before you use this schedule. A nearby Aurora or county address has a different counter. The property search and the building department, at 303-754-3321 on the review page, are the City's tools. This website does not look up the parcel for you.
