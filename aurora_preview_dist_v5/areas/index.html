Nearby cities, when the rules differ

Aurora's sewer tap rule does not follow you across a city limit. These cities were written from their own permit or utility pages. A town that is only a point on a map is not given Aurora's sentences.

  • Centennial does not provide water or sewer. A house water heater is one-stop. An addition is not. See also Centennial water heater replacement.
  • Parker permits inside the town. The district's service-line boundary is not settled on that page.
  • Highlands Ranch uses Douglas County, not a city building department.
  • Castle Rock reviews tankless heaters with a gas-load sheet. See Castle Rock tankless installation.
  • Lakewood has many districts. The city sewer graphic is only the north-side utility.
  • Arvada says you maintain the service line on your property, and stops there.
  • Englewood permits water heaters and water-line changes. A 2024 ownership proposal was not treated as current law.
  • Littleton owners keep the sewer line including the tap. Denver Water supplies drinking water.
  • Westminster owners keep the lateral to the main, including under a driveway.
  • Denver keeps a house water heater on a quick permit and the sewer lateral, including the tap, on the owner.
  • Thornton uses a different permit when a service repair enters the right of way.
  • Northglenn lists water heaters and sewers as one-stop permits.
  • Commerce City splits building permits from right-of-way permits.
  • Brighton allows an emergency heater or service repair only if you permit it the next business day and leave it open.
  • Federal Heights sends electrical permits to the state.
  • Wheat Ridge permits water heater replacement in OpenGov.
  • Broomfield permits every water heater, and it will not issue a new tap license before the building permit.
  • Louisville can review a water heater the same day. An addition takes weeks.
  • Longmont uses different phones for taps inside and outside the building.
  • Greenwood Village makes you find the district on a map before the permit.
  • Lone Tree does not sell water. Southgate, Parker Water, and Northern Douglas County Water can each apply. The bill controls. The building permit stays with the city.
  • Castle Pines has its own building department. The water district is whoever bills the house.
  • Edgewater runs its water utility and splits sewer with East Jefferson Sanitation District.
  • Sheridan requires a water-heater permit visible from the street.
  • Foxfield is its own town, with water heaters on a one-stop list.
  • Bow Mar files building permits through Columbine Valley.
  • Columbine Valley is the building counter Bow Mar uses.
  • Cherry Hills Village has a sanitation district, and some taps are also inspected by Englewood.
  • Watkins is a census place in both Adams County and Arapahoe County. The ZIP code is not the permit office.
  • Bennett is a town in both Adams County and Arapahoe County. Mail can fall outside the town.
  • Strasburg is a census place in both Adams County and Arapahoe County, not a city.
  • Byers is a census place in Arapahoe County, not Adams County. A Byers ZIP still needs a parcel check.
  • Elizabeth permits only inside town. Many Elizabeth addresses are Elbert County.
  • Cherry Creek CDP is Arapahoe County, not Denver's Cherry Creek neighborhood.
  • Four Square Mile is an Arapahoe County census area, not Aurora.
  • Franktown is Douglas County, and it is not on Pinery Water's community list.
  • Sedalia has a water district whose primary published service is water, not a census-wide sewer.
  • The Pinery is Pinery Water's community, not a stand-in for the other Douglas places.
  • Stonegate is Stonegate Village Metropolitan District where the bill says so, not the Town of Parker.
  • Meridian is Meridian Metropolitan District for its customers, not the City of Lone Tree.
  • Roxborough Park is Douglas County. Roxborough Water's central, east, and west areas are not the same service.
  • Evergreen uses Jefferson County, with a well or a district proved on Form 1001.
  • Golden permits water heaters and water or sewer utility repairs, after an address check that the municipal status is Golden.
  • Morrison contracts inspections to Charles Abbott Associates. Confirm the utility on the bill before street work.
  • Erie reviews a water heater in one to two business days.
  • Lafayette distinguishes a water-heater repair from a replacement.
  • Superior takes permits only through CommunityCore. SAFEbuilt inspects.
  • Boulder treats water heaters as mechanical permits and can exempt a simple stoppage.

A Census point within 50 straight-line miles is not a promise that a plumber will come, and it is not drive time. Denver's lead program is a guide, not a city hub, because eligibility is an address search. If your mail says Aurora and the parcel is not, confirm the city before you use Aurora's sewer page.
