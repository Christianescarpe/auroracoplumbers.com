Hydro-jetting in Aurora

Hydro-jetting uses water at high pressure to cut roots and grease. It can be the right cleaning for a service line. It can also be the wrong tool in a pipe that is already cracked. Aurora gives you a second, unrelated meaning of "jetting": the city truck flushing its own main in the street.

Your line is not the city main

Aurora Water's wastewater page describes a large jet truck working the city sewer. You may hear the engine, and you may hear gurgling in toilets and sinks. The City says the nozzle's pressure in the main can pull water from traps and that the water returns when the pass is done. It also says sewage from the main is not being pushed into the house by that work. Keep traps full so sewer gas stays out.

That operation does not jet your service line, and it does not mean you hired hydro-jetting. If a backup continues after the city main is flowing, the next question is the owner-maintained line from the tap to the house. Read sewer line repair for who pays, and camera inspection for how you see the pipe.

When a hired jet makes sense

A cable punches a hole in a clog. A jet washes the wall of the pipe, which is why it is used on grease and on fine roots. It belongs after a camera, or at least after the operator can say the pipe is intact enough to take the pressure. The City's note that pre-1985 homes can have clay is a reason to look, because a fractured clay pipe does not need more force. PVC is not automatically safe either. Offsets and bellies still matter.

A jetting quote should name the access cleanout, the length to be washed, and what is excluded. It should not promise the line is repaired. Roots return to moisture at a crack. The service-line page says an intact pipe is less inviting to roots and suggests cleaning a problem line, but cleaning on a schedule is not a patch. If the video shows a break, go back to repair or replacement.

Drain cleaning with a small cable is still the better first step for one indoor fixture. Jetting a kitchen branch from a roof vent, without knowing where the nozzle will go, is how fixtures get flooded. Ask where the hose enters and where it stops.

Key Clarifications

Questions to settle first

Is the city jet truck cleaning my service line?

No. The truck described on Aurora's wastewater page is flushing the city main. Gurgling in your toilet can be that work. It is not a visit you scheduled, and it does not restore a broken lateral. If the backup remains, the service line from the tap to the house is still the owner's problem.

Should the line be jetted before it is camera'd?

Usually no. Jetting is a strong cleaning, and a cracked or offset pipe can be made worse. Ask for a camera unless the operator can already show a recent recording of this run. Roots in an otherwise round pipe are a better candidate than a collapse.

Will jetting stop roots for good?

It removes the roots it can reach. The City notes that roots follow moisture into a crack or a separated joint. If the video shows that opening, jetting is maintenance until the joint is repaired. A quote that calls jetting a permanent fix, without a repair, is overselling the hose.

How is this different from cabling a drain?

A cable opens a path through a clog and is the usual tool for one slow fixture. A jet washes the pipe wall and is aimed at grease and roots in a larger line. Neither one replaces a crushed section. Pick the tool after you know whether the pipe is blocked, broken, or both.
